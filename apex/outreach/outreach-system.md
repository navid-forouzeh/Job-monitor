# APEX Outbound Acquisition System
## Complete Playbook · LinkedIn + Email · Navid Forouzeh, Zürich

*Tone standard: Short. Human. Premium. Peer-to-peer. Not needy. Not salesy. Never desperate.*

---

## 1. IDEAL CLIENT PROFILES

### Profile A — Pre-Seed Founder Actively Fundraising

**Who they are:** Founders 6–18 months into building, approaching or actively in their first institutional raise. They have a product and early traction. They do not have a professional pitch deck. They may not know that their story is the problem.

**LinkedIn search criteria:**
- Job title filters: "Co-Founder," "CEO & Co-Founder," "Founder & CEO," "Founding Partner"
- Keywords in profile: "Pre-Seed," "raising," "investors," "FinTech," "SaaS," "deeptech," "healthtech," "climate," "B2B"
- Company size: 1–10 employees
- Company age: Founded 2022–2025 (use LinkedIn "Year founded" filter)
- Geography: Switzerland, Germany, Austria — prioritize Zürich, Basel, Munich, Berlin, Vienna
- Languages: English and/or German profile

**Prospect signals (reasons to reach out now):**
- Recent LinkedIn post about fundraising or investor meetings
- Company page says "Hiring" (means they are scaling)
- Profile updated recently (active on platform — will see the message)
- Just completed an accelerator program (Venture Kick, Y Combinator, Swiss Startup Factory, F10)
- Mentioned "pitch deck" in a post or comment

**Disqualifiers:** Already at Series A or beyond (they have internal resources), B2C consumer app (wrong context), non-DACH geography

---

### Profile B — Series A/B Scale-Up Executive Needing Board or Investor Materials

**Who they are:** CTOs, COOs, Heads of Strategy, VP Finance at scale-ups that have already raised institutional money and are running quarterly board meetings or preparing for their next raise. The pitch materials are now board decks and Series A/B investor updates. The stakes are higher, the audience is more sophisticated, and the need for professional-grade communication is more acute.

**LinkedIn search criteria:**
- Job title filters: "Chief Operating Officer," "Chief Technology Officer," "VP Strategy," "Head of Finance," "VP Investor Relations," "Chief of Staff"
- Keywords: "Series A," "Series B," "board," "investors," "scale-up," "growth stage"
- Company size: 25–200 employees
- Geography: Zürich, Basel, Munich, Berlin, Vienna
- Seniority: Director level and above

**Prospect signals:**
- Company recently announced a funding round (check Crunchbase, Swiss Startup Radar, EU-Startups)
- LinkedIn post about "hiring a communications lead" or "looking for a storyteller"
- Profile mentions "board meetings," "investor reporting," "Series A prep"
- Company is 12–24 months past their last raise (starting to think about the next one)
- Job posting for a "Strategy" or "Finance" role at the company (means they are building out the function)

**Disqualifiers:** Large corporates with internal corporate communications teams (wrong audience), early-stage startups (belongs in Profile A)

---

### Profile C — Swiss KMU / SME Owner Seeking Financing or Partnerships

**Who they are:** Second- or third-generation family business owners, or founder-CEOs of established Swiss SMEs (20–500 employees) pursuing bank financing, strategic partnerships, or investor introductions. Conservative audience, traditional business context, high trust threshold. German-language communication preferred.

**LinkedIn search criteria:**
- Job title filters: "Geschäftsführer," "CEO," "Inhaber," "Managing Director," "Founder"
- Keywords: "Schweizer Unternehmen," "Mittelstand," "Expansion," "Wachstum," "Finanzierung"
- Company size: 20–500 employees
- Industries: Manufacturing, engineering, professional services, logistics, construction technology
- Geography: German-speaking Switzerland — Zürich, Winterthur, Basel, Bern, St. Gallen
- Company age: 10+ years (established businesses, not startups)

**Prospect signals:**
- LinkedIn post about expansion plans, new facilities, or new markets
- Company hiring for senior roles (means the business is growing)
- Profile mentions "Bankgespräch," "Investoren," "strategische Partnerschaft"
- Recently featured in Handelszeitung, NZZ, or regional Swiss business press
- Attended or speaking at a Swiss industry association event

**Disqualifiers:** Pure consumer businesses, companies in financial distress (wrong positioning for APEX), public companies (have IR resources)

## 2. WHERE TO FIND THEM

### LinkedIn Sales Navigator Searches

**Search 1 — Active Swiss Fundraisers:**
Title: "Co-Founder" OR "CEO" · Geography: Switzerland · Company size: 1–10 · Keywords: "raising" OR "pre-seed" OR "pitch" · Posted in last 30 days. Save as alert. Review weekly.

**Search 2 — DACH Scale-Up Executives:**
Title: "COO" OR "CFO" OR "VP Finance" OR "Head of Strategy" · Geography: Switzerland, Germany, Austria · Company size: 25–200 · Keywords: "Series A" OR "Series B" OR "board" · Seniority: Director+.

**Search 3 — Swiss SME Owners:**
Title: "Geschäftsführer" OR "Inhaber" OR "Managing Director" · Geography: Zürich, Basel, Bern, Winterthur · Company size: 20–500 · Industry: Manufacturing, Engineering Services, Technology.

**Search 4 — Recently Funded Companies:**
Use "Past Company" filter to find founders who left a funded startup (indicates startup ecosystem familiarity). Cross-reference with Crunchbase to find Swiss companies funded in last 6 months.

### LinkedIn Groups and Communities to Join

- Swiss Startups & Entrepreneurs (largest Swiss startup group on LinkedIn)
- Swiss Tech Circle
- DACH Founders Network
- FinTech Switzerland
- Swiss Digital Health Network
- Zürich Entrepreneurs (local community group)
- Swiss Investors & Angels Network (monitor for recently funded startups)

*Strategy: Join groups to observe, comment thoughtfully on relevant posts, and build presence before cold outreach. A profile that has engaged meaningfully in a shared group converts better than a completely cold approach.*

### Swiss Startup Events and Accelerators

**Venture Kick (venturekick.ch):** Switzerland's largest startup competition. Winners announced publicly — reach out to recent finalists and winners immediately after announcement. They are actively fundraising and have just received public validation.

**Swiss Startup Factory (swissstartupfactory.com):** Zürich-based accelerator. Follow their cohort announcements. Reach out to graduating cohort members at demo day time.

**F10 FinTech & InsurTech (f10.global):** Zürich and Singapore-based accelerator for FinTech and InsurTech. Cohort companies are almost always in active fundraising mode. Monitor their website and LinkedIn page.

**Impact Hub Zürich (zurich.impacthub.net):** Swiss community for impact entrepreneurs. Strong network of purpose-driven founders who value communication. Events are open and public — attend to meet prospects in person first, which dramatically improves outreach conversion.

**EPFL Innovation Park (epfl.ch/innovation-park):** Lausanne-based tech cluster. Deep tech, biotech, and engineering startups. Many founders are technically excellent and communication-weak — ideal APEX prospect.

**Swiss Startup Days:** Annual conference in Bern. Sponsor or attend; list of presenting companies is public.

**Startupticker.ch:** The definitive Swiss startup news platform. Monitor for funding announcements, company news, and founder profiles. A funding announcement is a direct trigger for outreach.

### Newsletters and Platforms Where Founders Are Active

- **Swiss Startup Radar (startupradar.ch):** Newsletter tracking Swiss startup ecosystem. Funding rounds listed with founder names.
- **EU-Startups (eu-startups.com):** Covers DACH funding rounds. Set up alerts for Switzerland, Germany, Austria.
- **Crunchbase:** Set alerts for new Swiss funding rounds under $5M (Pre-Seed/Seed territory).
- **Dealroom.co:** European startup database with funding data. Good for finding Series A/B companies 12–24 months post-raise.
- **LinkedIn Newsletter notifications:** Follow influential Swiss startup accounts (Venturelab, Innosuisse) and watch for engagement in the comments — active commenters are active on the platform.

## 12. CALL BOOKING CTA LANGUAGE

*3 versions for different contexts. Never "30-minute strategy session." Never "free consultation." These phrases signal freelancer energy, not premium advisor energy.*

---

**Version 1 — Low-pressure, peer framing:**
> "If any of this resonates, a 20-minute call is enough to know if there's anything useful here — no preparation required on your end."

**Version 2 — Specific trigger framing (for someone in an active process):**
> "If you're heading into investor meetings in the next month or two, it's worth a 20-minute call to see if there's a quick win on the framing before that starts."

**Version 3 — Soft curiosity framing (for someone early-stage):**
> "Happy to share how I've seen this done well at your stage — takes 20 minutes and there's no obligation on either side."

*Booking link language: "Book a 20-minute call" — not "schedule a consultation," not "book a strategy session," not "get a free audit." Simple, direct, time-bounded.*

## 13. VOICE NOTE SCRIPT

*60 seconds. LinkedIn voice note. Effective because almost nobody does it. Sounds human, not scripted.*

**What to say:**

> "Hey [FIRST NAME] — Navid here. I sent you a quick note earlier this week and figured a voice message might be more human than another line of text.
>
> I came across [COMPANY NAME] — genuinely find what you're building in [SPACE] interesting. And I wanted to share one honest observation: from the outside, the story you're telling investors doesn't quite match the strength of what the company actually is. That gap is usually the difference between a pitch that generates meetings and one that doesn't.
>
> I work with founders in Switzerland on exactly this — pitch architecture, investor narrative, the structure of how you communicate a company that's worth betting on.
>
> Anyway — no pressure at all. If the timing ever makes sense, I'm easy to find. Hope the week's going well."

**What NOT to say:**
- Do not read from a script — it sounds like a script
- Do not mention pricing, packages, or your process
- Do not say "I'd love to jump on a call" — it sounds eager
- Do not open with your title or a formal introduction — it kills the human tone immediately
- Do not use the word "leverage," "synergy," "value-add," or "solutions"
- Do not be longer than 70 seconds — longer and the person feels cornered

**Delivery notes:**
Record in a quiet space. Speak at a slightly slower pace than normal conversation. Smile while recording — it comes through in the voice. One take is usually better than a polished re-take — it sounds less like a sales script.

## SYSTEM NOTES

**Volume guidance:**
- Send 10–15 outreach messages per week maximum. Premium positioning requires selectivity. Spray-and-pray volume signals desperation and trains you to send generic messages.
- Focus on 5 highly personalized messages per week over 15 generic ones. Personalization requires reading their LinkedIn page, their company website, and one recent post before reaching out.

**Tracking:**
Maintain a simple CRM (even a Notion table) with: Name, Company, Platform (LinkedIn/Email), Message Version, Date Sent, Response (Y/N), Follow-Up 1 Date, Follow-Up 2 Date, Status.

**Response rate expectations:**
- Cold LinkedIn DM to warm prospect (post-engagement, shared context): 15–25% response
- Cold LinkedIn DM to cold prospect (researched, personalized): 8–15% response
- Cold email (personalized): 5–12% open-to-reply rate
- Voice note follow-up: Higher than text — 20–30% response when combined with a prior message

**Conversion from response to call:**
- Expect 30–40% of responses to convert to a discovery call with good follow-up
- Expect 20–30% of calls to convert to a scoped engagement

**The mindset standard:**
Every message sent under the APEX name should be one that Navid would be comfortable having the recipient share publicly. If a message feels slightly pushy, slightly generic, or slightly transactional — it does not go out. The reputation built by restraint and quality is worth more than the lead generated by a marginal message.
