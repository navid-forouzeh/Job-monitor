## FILE REFERENCE: PURPOSE, USE, AND UPDATE CADENCE

---

### `brand/`

---

#### `brand/positioning.md`

**Purpose:** The single source of truth for how APEX is positioned in the market. Contains: one-sentence positioning statement, target client segments (with specifics on company stage and size), the core problem APEX solves, how APEX is differentiated from alternatives, and the "what I do" paragraph used across LinkedIn, the website, and outreach.

**When to use it:** Before writing any new copy — website, outreach, LinkedIn, proposals. Pull language directly from this file rather than reinventing it each time. Share it with anyone helping you with copy or marketing.

**How often to update it:** Review monthly. Update only when you have evidence (from client conversations or market response) that the positioning is not landing. Do not change it based on how you feel on a given day — change it based on data.

---

#### `brand/name-analysis.md`

**Purpose:** Documents the naming decision for APEX — the alternatives considered, the rationale for the final choice, and any legal or trademark considerations (particularly for Swiss business registration if you formalize APEX as a legal entity). Also contains domain options considered and the one registered.

**When to use it:** If you ever revisit the brand name (unlikely, but possible), this file reminds you what was already evaluated and why the current name was chosen. Saves re-doing work.

**How often to update it:** Rarely. Update only if you rebrand, register a legal entity, or add a new domain.

---

#### `brand/visual-direction.md`

**Purpose:** Documents the complete visual identity of APEX. Contains: primary and accent color hex codes, font names and where to download them, spacing principles, slide layout rules (for showcases and client work), logo treatment (if any), and examples of "on-brand" vs "off-brand" design decisions. This file is your style guide.

**When to use it:** Every time you build a new showcase, create a client presentation, design website updates, or produce any branded asset (LinkedIn banner, PDF header, email signature).

**How often to update it:** Update when you make a deliberate brand refinement (e.g., change accent color, add a logo). Do not update it casually — visual consistency is a credibility signal.

---

### `offers/`

---

#### `offers/offer-structure.md`

**Purpose:** The complete, detailed description of all three APEX service offers. For each offer: name, tagline, target client, problem it solves, what the client receives (deliverables), timeline, price in CHF, payment terms, and what's explicitly excluded (scope boundaries). This is the internal reference — the proposal template translates this into client-facing language.

**When to use it:** When writing a proposal, when explaining your services in a sales call, when updating the website services section, when someone asks "what exactly do you do?" and you need precise language.

**How often to update it:** Review quarterly. Update prices when you have 3+ completed client engagements — at that point, raise them. Update deliverables if client feedback consistently shows a mismatch between what you promise and what you deliver.

---

#### `offers/pricing-philosophy.md`

**Purpose:** Documents the reasoning behind your pricing — not just the numbers, but the principles. Why you price at CHF X and not CHF Y. Why you use 50% upfront. Why you don't offer hourly rates. Why you have 3 tiers and not 5. This is for you — to stay anchored when clients push back on price or when you feel tempted to discount.

**When to use it:** Before any negotiation. Before lowering a price. When you're second-guessing your rates. When onboarding a new client who questions the investment.

**How often to update it:** Update when your market understanding changes materially — e.g., after 10 completed projects you have real data on what clients value most, and your pricing philosophy should reflect that.

---

### `website/`

---

#### `website/landing-page-copy.md`

**Purpose:** The complete, approved copy for the APEX website — every headline, subheadline, body paragraph, CTA label, section description, and footer text. Organized by section (Hero, Services, Process, Showcases, Contact, Footer). This is the master copy document. All website text should match this file.

**When to use it:** When building or updating the website. When reviewing copy. When briefing a designer or developer if you hire help. When adapting website copy for other channels (proposal intros, LinkedIn about section, email signatures).

**How often to update it:** Review when you change your positioning or offers. Update copy whenever you refine messaging based on market feedback (e.g., a section that generates no engagement may need a rewrite). Do not change copy on a whim — change it when you have a reason.

---

#### `website/build-instructions.md`

**Purpose:** Step-by-step instructions for managing the APEX Framer website — how to log in, how to edit specific sections, how to add showcase cards, how to republish, how to update the custom domain, and how to enable or read analytics. Written for future-you who may not remember how Framer works after weeks away.

**When to use it:** Every time you update the website. When troubleshooting a broken link, layout issue, or domain problem. If you ever bring in help to maintain the site.

**How often to update it:** Update when you change platforms, update the domain setup, or add new features to the site. Keep it accurate — a wrong instruction is worse than no instruction.

Contains the approved disclaimer text (for website footer, PDF first pages, and any public sharing), the reasoning behind using fictional companies (portfolio building while protecting client confidentiality), and notes on how to handle it if a prospect asks about real client work.

**When to use it:** Copy disclaimer text from here whenever publishing or sharing showcase work. Reference the reasoning when a prospect asks whether your showcases are from real clients.

**How often to update it:** Review if you get legal advice suggesting a more robust disclaimer is needed. Update if you add real client work to the website (then the disclaimer scope changes).

### `outreach/`

---

#### `outreach/outreach-system.md`

**Purpose:** The complete outreach playbook — how APEX does cold outreach. Covers: which channels to use (LinkedIn, email, warm intro), which target segments to prioritize, how to find prospect contact information, daily and weekly outreach volume targets, what to track (and how), and how to improve the system over time based on response data.

**When to use it:** Every time you do outreach. When reviewing what's working and what isn't. When training yourself on a new channel or tactic. When your response rate drops and you need to diagnose why.

**How often to update it:** Review monthly. Update based on data — if a channel is generating responses, document what's working. If something isn't working, note what you tried and why you changed it.

---

#### `outreach/prospect-list.md`

**Purpose:** A reference document for how to build and maintain your prospect list — search criteria, sources (LinkedIn search, accelerator portals, investor databases, event attendee lists), how to qualify a prospect, and how to prioritize outreach order. The actual list lives in Notion or Google Sheets, but this file documents the methodology.

**When to use it:** When expanding your prospect list. When a batch of outreach runs dry and you need to find new contacts. When your targeting isn't converting and you need to rethink who you're reaching out to.

**How often to update it:** Update when you discover a new effective source of prospects, or when you decide to change your target segment.

---

#### `outreach/message-templates.md`

**Purpose:** All approved outreach message templates — the initial connection message, the follow-up message, the partnership pitch (for accelerators), the warm referral request, and any templates developed for specific segments (founders vs. executives vs. investors). Each template includes: the version number, the date it was last tested, and the response rate if known.

**When to use it:** Every time you write an outreach message — pull from this file and personalize, rather than writing from scratch. When A/B testing a new message angle, document both versions here.

**How often to update it:** Update whenever you write a new template or revise an existing one. Always note the date and your reason for changing it.

### `sales/`

---

#### `sales/sales-call-script.md`

**Purpose:** The discovery call framework — not a rigid script, but a structured guide. Contains: how to open the call (30 seconds), the 5–7 discovery questions to ask, how to listen for buying signals, how to explain APEX's offer in response to their situation (not in a generic pitch), how to handle common objections (price, timeline, uncertainty about ROI), and how to close toward a next step. Includes a post-call review checklist.

**When to use it:** Before every sales or discovery call — review the script, prepare your discovery questions specific to that prospect, and write down what you want to learn from them. After every call — review what worked and what you'd do differently.

**How often to update it:** Update after every 3–5 calls. You will learn things from real conversations that no template could anticipate. The script should evolve as your sales ability improves.

---

#### `sales/proposal-template.md`

**Purpose:** The master proposal template — a single-page or two-page document that can be customized for any prospect. Structure: Their Situation (proof that you listened and understood), Recommended Approach (which offer and why), Deliverables + Timeline, Investment (price in CHF), Payment Terms, Next Steps. This file also contains guidance on how to customize each section for each client, and notes on common mistakes to avoid in proposals (being too generic, burying the price, vague deliverables).

**When to use it:** After every positive discovery call — copy this template, fill it in based on your call notes, and send it within 24 hours while the conversation is fresh. The faster you send, the higher your close rate.

**How often to update it:** Update after every proposal you send — note what you changed and whether it helped close the deal. After 10 proposals, you'll know what structure converts best.

### `delivery/`

---

#### `delivery/project-workflow.md`

**Purpose:** The standard workflow for delivering a client project — from signed proposal to final delivery. Step by step: kickoff questionnaire sent, kickoff call, narrative brief (if applicable), first draft shared, revision round, final delivery, offboarding and feedback request. Includes time estimates for each step and notes on client communication rhythm (when to update them, how proactively to share work in progress).

**When to use it:** At the start of every client project — review this to make sure you're running the engagement correctly. When something goes wrong mid-project (client is unclear, timeline slips, scope creeps) — reference this to see where the breakdown happened.

**How often to update it:** Update after each completed project. Client feedback will reveal steps that need to be added, removed, or changed.

---

#### `delivery/quality-checklist.md`

**Purpose:** The pre-delivery review checklist for every deck or narrative produced for a client. Items include: narrative logic (does the story flow? Is the argument clear?), visual consistency (colors, fonts, spacing, alignment), data accuracy (are all numbers internally consistent?), language quality (spelling, grammar, clarity, no filler phrases), format (correct file types delivered, correct naming convention, PDF and editable version if agreed), and client-specific requirements (anything promised in the proposal that must be present).

**When to use it:** Before sending any deliverable to a client — go through this checklist line by line. Do not skip it, even if you're confident in the work. The checklist catches things your eye misses after hours of working on the same file.

**How often to update it:** Update whenever a delivery error occurs — add the specific check that would have caught it.

### `ai-prompts/`

---

#### `ai-prompts/production-prompts.md`

**Purpose:** A curated library of AI prompts used in the production of APEX work — organized by use case. Categories: pitch deck narrative development, slide-by-slide content generation, board presentation structuring, executive memo drafting, outreach message personalization, LinkedIn post drafting, objection handling for sales calls, and proposal writing. Each prompt includes: the prompt text, the context it requires (what to paste in alongside it), and notes on how to use the output (always edit, never paste raw).

**When to use it:** At the start of any production task — check if an existing prompt covers your need before writing a new one. When a prompt works well, note it here. When a prompt fails consistently, delete it or revise it.

**How often to update it:** Update continuously as you develop new prompts. This is a living document and one of the highest-value files in the APEX system — a strong prompt library is a competitive advantage.

#### `content/linkedin-strategy.md`

**Purpose:** The complete LinkedIn content strategy for APEX — target audience (on LinkedIn, not just in general), content pillars (the 3–5 themes you post about), post formats that work for this audience (insight posts, mini case studies, opinion pieces, process breakdowns), optimal posting frequency, best times to post in the DACH market, and how to engage with the community beyond posting. #### `content/post-archive/`

**Purpose:** A folder containing every LinkedIn post published by APEX, saved as individual markdown files. Each file named: `YYYY-MM-DD-post-topic.md`. Inside each file: the full post text, the date published, the engagement metrics (likes, comments, shares, impressions — copy from LinkedIn analytics), and a brief note on what worked or didn't.

**When to use it:** When looking for a post to repurpose or update. When reviewing what content has performed best (to inform future strategy). When a prospect asks for writing samples and you want to share a post quickly.

**How often to update it:** Add a new file every time a post is published. Update metrics at 7 days and 30 days after publishing — these are the meaningful data points.

## APEX OPERATING RHYTHM

The operating rhythm is how APEX runs as a business — not just how it launches. These are the recurring review and production cycles.

---

### WEEKLY (every Friday or Sunday evening, 60–90 minutes)

**Review**
- Open the Leads + Outreach database in Notion — update every lead's status, log any new responses, set follow-up reminders for anyone who needs a touch next week
- Count weekly KPIs: messages sent, responses received, calls booked, proposals sent, revenue closed
- Review LinkedIn analytics: which post performed best this week? What's your follower count? Who engaged?
- Check website analytics (Framer or GA4): sessions, bounce rate, which pages got traffic

**Update**
- Update `content/post-archive/` with any posts published this week (add engagement metrics at 7 days)
- Update `outreach/message-templates.md` if you tested a new message version and got a result
- Update your Notion Projects database if any client projects progressed or completed

**Create**
- Draft next week's LinkedIn posts (aim for 2 posts drafted ahead of time — reduces friction and improves quality)
- Identify the next batch of outreach prospects (15–20 for the coming week)
- Write or refine 1 AI prompt that you'll use in next week's production work

---

### MONTHLY (last day of each month, 2–3 hours)

**Assess**
- Run through the Month Honest Assessment Framework from `execution/30-day-plan.md` (or an adapted version for Month 2 onwards)
- Review revenue: how much did APEX generate? What's in the pipeline? What's the trend?
- Review content performance: which content pillar got the most traction? Should you increase frequency, change topics, or change format?
- Review outreach: what is your cumulative response rate? Which segments are converting best?
- Ask: is the offer still right? Is the pricing still right? Is the target segment still right? Change only if you have clear evidence — not feelings.

**Archive**
- Move completed project files to an "Archive" subfolder within `delivery/`
- Archive LinkedIn posts from `content/post-archive/` that are more than 90 days old into a "Yearly Archive" subfolder
- Archive the outreach messages from the previous month into a monthly subfolder in `outreach/` — keep your active tracker clean

**Plan**
- Write next month's plan (even if brief — a page in Notion is enough): key goals, primary outreach strategy, content focus, any new initiatives (partnerships, referrals, PR)
- Set the next month's KPI targets based on Month 1 actuals — targets should be achievable but require growth
- Identify 1 thing to improve this month that would have the largest impact on revenue: fix it, don't just note it
