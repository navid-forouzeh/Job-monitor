# Showcase 01 — Investor Pitch Deck
## NovaPay AG · Pre-Seed · CHF 2.5M

**Type:** Investor Pitch Deck
**Stage:** Pre-Seed
**Raise:** CHF 2.5M
**Industry:** FinTech / B2B Payment Infrastructure
**Location:** Zürich, Switzerland
**Engagement:** Full Pitch Architecture · Narrative Development · Slide System · Visual Direction

---

## 1. CLIENT CONTEXT

NovaPay AG is a Zürich-based B2B FinTech founded in early 2025 by four co-founders: two software engineers who previously built payment infrastructure at SIX Group, a former UBS corporate banking director with seven years of Swiss treasury advisory experience, and an ex-McKinsey consultant who led digital transformation engagements for Swiss financial institutions. The company had spent six months building a working product and onboarding twelve pilot customers before deciding to formalize their fundraising process. At the time of engagement with APEX, they had CHF 180K in ARR with consistent 15% month-over-month growth — enough to demonstrate early product-market fit, but not enough to make the numbers the story. They needed language, structure, and architecture to make investors feel what the numbers alone could not communicate.

The founding team came to APEX with a clear problem: they were technically exceptional and operationally credible, but their existing pitch materials were built by engineers for engineers. The draft deck they shared was 22 slides long, led with a product walkthrough, buried the market opportunity on slide fourteen, and used the phrase "seamless integration" four times. They had a strong business. They did not have a strong story. APEX was engaged to rebuild the pitch from first principles — not to repackage what existed, but to construct the strongest possible case for why NovaPay is the obvious bet in Swiss SME payment infrastructure right now.

---

## 2. THE CHALLENGE

Before APEX, the NovaPay pitch suffered from three structural problems that are common among technical founding teams.

**Problem 1: The product led, not the problem.** The original deck opened with a product demo walkthrough. This is almost always a mistake in early-stage fundraising. Investors do not buy products — they buy markets, teams, and timing. Opening with product features before establishing the scale of the pain guarantees a cold room. The investor's first question is always "why does this matter?" — and in the original deck, it was not being answered until slide nine.

**Problem 2: The market slide was unsupported.** The team had written "Swiss SME payments — CHF 12B market." That number was not sourced, not segmented, and not made credible. There was no distinction between TAM, SAM, and SOM, and no explanation of how NovaPay could realistically capture a portion of that figure. Sophisticated investors dismiss unsupported market sizing immediately — it signals that the founders have not done the analytical work.

**Problem 3: The traction was buried and undersold.** Twelve pilot customers and CHF 180K ARR is meaningful early validation. But the deck presented it as a bulleted list after the business model slide. There was no visual, no growth rate chart, no customer references (even anonymized), and no narrative context for what 15% MoM growth means compounded. The team had proof that their bet was paying off — and they were not using it.

---

## 3. APEX APPROACH

APEX began the engagement with a two-hour strategy session focused not on slides but on investor psychology. The core question was: what does a Pre-Seed investor in the DACH region need to believe in order to wire CHF 2.5M? The answer shaped every decision that followed.

**Narrative decision 1: Lead with the human moment, not the market size.** The deck was restructured to open with a single real scenario — a Swiss SME owner spending her Saturday afternoon reconciling payment records, matching invoices across three banking portals, manually correcting errors before her accountant arrived on Monday. This is not a hypothetical. This is the weekly reality for over 600,000 Swiss SMEs. Grounding the pitch in a concrete human experience before introducing market data makes the problem feel urgent, not abstract.

**Narrative decision 2: Make traction the emotional climax, not a middle-of-deck data dump.** In the restructured deck, traction appears on slide 7 — right after the product demonstration and business model. By this point, the investor understands what NovaPay does and how it makes money. The traction slide then lands as confirmation: this is not theoretical. This is happening. The 15% MoM growth chart was built to show compounding visually — what CHF 180K becomes at the same growth rate in 18 months. This reframes traction from "current state" to "proof of trajectory."

**Narrative decision 3: Position the team as the unfair advantage.** In a crowded FinTech market, team slides are often defensive ("here are our qualifications"). APEX rebuilt the NovaPay team slide to be offensive — framing the specific combination of the UBS director's banking relationships, the SIX Group engineers' infrastructure experience, and the McKinsey consultant's enterprise sales playbook as a competitive moat that cannot be easily replicated. The question the slide answers is not "are these people qualified?" but "why are these four people the only ones who could build this?"

## 4. SLIDE OUTLINE

**Slide 1 — Cover**
NovaPay wordmark on a deep navy background. Single-line tagline: "Payment infrastructure built for Swiss business." A restrained, full-bleed design with a data-grid inspired background pattern connoting precision and infrastructure. The cover is clean and confident — it does not attempt to explain the business. It signals that the deck is worth opening.

**Slide 2 — The Problem**
A single visual anchoring the viewer in a specific moment: a Swiss SME finance manager's screen at 11pm, three browser tabs open across three banking portals, a reconciliation spreadsheet active, and 47 unmatched transactions pending. Headline: "Swiss SMEs lose 10 hours every week to payments. Not because of bad software. Because no one built the right one." No bullet points. The problem is made visceral before it is made statistical. This slide creates the tension the rest of the deck resolves.

**Slide 3 — Market Size**
A three-circle TAM/SAM/SOM visualization built from credible, cited sources (SECO SME reports, Swiss Payments Monitor 2024, SNB payment statistics). TAM: 600,000+ Swiss SMEs generating over CHF 4B in payment processing activity annually. SAM: the 180,000 SMEs with 5–50 employees and active digital accounting adoption. SOM: the 8,000 SMEs reachable via NovaPay's direct and channel sales strategy in 36 months. All numbers are sourced and footnoted. The framing: "We are not trying to replace Swiss banking. We are solving the last mile."

**Slide 4 — Solution**
One sentence, then one product schematic. "NovaPay connects every Swiss payment channel — PostFinance, UBS, Raiffeisen, credit cards, TWINT — into a single reconciliation layer that updates in real time." The schematic shows the integration architecture simply: bank logos on the left, a single NovaPay hub in the center, and the client's accounting system on the right. No feature list. No bullet points. The investor needs to understand what it does in 20 seconds.

**Slide 5 — Product (with Screenshot Placeholder)**
A deeper product walkthrough with two annotated screenshots: the automated matching engine and the one-click export to Swiss accounting standards (MWST-compliant). [SCREENSHOT PLACEHOLDER — Unified Reconciliation Dashboard] [SCREENSHOT PLACEHOLDER — Automated Matching Engine View]. Brief annotation on each screenshot explaining what the finance team sees vs. what they used to do manually. The narrative: what previously took 10 hours now takes under 20 minutes. Two customer quotes (anonymized at this stage) confirm the experience.

**Slide 6 — Business Model**
A clean two-part model illustration: a SaaS subscription tier diagram (CHF 149/month base, CHF 299/month professional, CHF 599/month enterprise) alongside a transaction fee layer (0.08% on cross-bank transfers above CHF 10,000/month). Average revenue per customer shown as a single calculated number. CAC vs. LTV ratio presented as a simple graphic, not a table. The framing: "We make more as our customers grow. Alignment by design."

**Slide 7 — Traction**
The emotional proof point. A line chart showing ARR growth from CHF 0 to CHF 180K over 8 months with the 15% MoM growth rate annotated and the 18-month forward projection implied by the trendline. Below: four large numbers with brief context sentences — 12 pilot customers, CHF 180K ARR, 15% MoM growth, 0% churn. One short customer quote (anonymized): "We got back two full working days per week. That pays for itself ten times over." The growth chart is clean and unadorned — the data does the work.

**Slide 8 — Go-to-Market**
A three-phase GTM strategy visualized on a 24-month timeline. Phase 1 (months 1–6): direct outbound to Zürich and Basel Mittelstand CFOs via the founding team's UBS and McKinsey networks. Phase 2 (months 7–18): channel partnerships with Swiss accounting software platforms — Abacus and Bexio — for integrated distribution. Phase 3 (months 18–24): self-serve onboarding funnel with inbound demand from content marketing and referral incentives. Each phase has a clear trigger metric before advancement. CAC and payback period projections included for each phase.

**Slide 9 — Competition**
A 2x2 competitive matrix positioning NovaPay on two axes: automation capability (y-axis) vs. Swiss banking integration depth (x-axis). Competitors plotted include Klarna Business, Stripe Invoicing, SumUp, and PostFinance's eBanking business module — all shown as partial solutions. NovaPay occupies the high-automation, high-Swiss-integration quadrant alone. Three-line narrative: "Global players don't understand Swiss banking compliance. Swiss banks don't build for SME usability. NovaPay is the only product built for both."

**Slide 10 — Team**
Four founder portraits, four "unfair advantage" statements. Above the portraits: "Built by the people who already understand both sides of Swiss payments." Each founder's one-line credential is framed as a market-specific asset, not a résumé entry. Below: advisory board reference — one current ZKB relationship manager and one former SIX Group board member. The question this slide answers is not "are these people qualified?" but "why can nobody else build this?"

**Slide 11 — Financials / Use of Funds**
A clear use-of-funds allocation chart: 45% engineering (two senior engineers + one product manager), 30% sales and GTM (two enterprise sales reps + growth marketing), 15% compliance and licensing (Swiss financial regulation readiness, PSD2-adjacent), 10% operations and runway buffer. Below: an 18-month financial projection showing the path from CHF 180K ARR to CHF 1.2M ARR, broken into new customer acquisition and existing customer expansion. Assumptions are conservative, clearly labeled, and footnoted. The slide communicates financial seriousness without overwhelming the room.

**Slide 12 — The Ask**
"We are raising CHF 2.5M Pre-Seed. This closes our compliance gap, triples the team, and puts us at CHF 1.2M ARR within 18 months." Three items below: what milestones this capital unlocks, what the Series A trigger looks like, and the investor profile NovaPay is building toward (Swiss-anchored, SME-infrastructure experience preferred). Current round status: CHF 800K soft-committed. Closing line: "We are not building another payment tool. We are building the payment backbone for Swiss business." Contact and next steps below.

## 5. NARRATIVE ARCHITECTURE NOTE

The NovaPay deck was built on what APEX calls the Tension–Proof–Momentum arc — a narrative structure designed specifically for Pre-Seed pitches where the evidence base is limited but the conviction must be high.

**Tension (Slides 1–3):** The deck opens by making the investor feel the problem before they understand the business. The Saturday-evening reconciliation scenario is visceral and recognizable to anyone who has worked with or in a Swiss SME finance function. The market sizing immediately follows to scale that feeling: this is not one woman's problem. This is 600,000 businesses losing collective millions of hours per year. The investor is leaning forward.

**Proof (Slides 4–7):** The middle of the deck delivers the answer: here is the product, here is how it makes money, and here is proof that it is already working. The sequence is deliberate — product before business model before traction, because each layer makes the next more credible. Traction without product context is just numbers. Traction after seeing the product is validation of a thesis.

**Momentum (Slides 8–12):** The final section reframes the investor's question from "should I bet on this?" to "how much should I put in before this closes?" GTM shows the acquisition machine is designed. Competition shows the white space is real and defensible. Team shows the people are uniquely positioned. Financials show capital is deployed wisely. The Ask closes the loop with confidence, not desperation — the round is moving, the door is open, and this is the moment to participate.

The deck was written to be read in 4 minutes cold or presented in 12 minutes live. Every slide carries one primary message. No slide competes with itself.

## 6. VISUAL STYLE DIRECTION

**Color Palette:** Deep navy (#0D1B2A) as the primary background for the cover, traction, and closing slides. Clean white (#FFFFFF) for content-heavy slides. A single accent color — electric teal (#00C2B2) — used only for data callouts, key metrics, and the company wordmark. Never for decoration. Muted warm grey (#F5F4F1) for secondary and divider pages. The discipline of a single accent color is what keeps the deck from looking like a startup template.

**Typography:** Headings in a geometric sans-serif (Neue Haas Grotesk or Aktiv Grotesk) — clean, Swiss, institutional without being cold. Body copy in a humanist sans-serif (Inter) for readability at all sizes. Type hierarchy is strict: 48pt headline, 24pt subhead, 16pt body, 11pt footnote. Nothing smaller than 11pt appears on any slide. If a point requires smaller text, it belongs in an appendix.

**Density:** Deliberately low. Each slide carries one primary visual and one primary message. The target is that a slide can be understood in under 5 seconds without reading a single bullet point. Charts are labeled directly on the data — no legends. Icons are used sparingly and only when they replace words, not when they decorate them. White space is used as a signal of confidence, not an absence of content.

**Image Approach:** No stock photography. Product screenshots are lightly shadowed and cropped tightly to show only the relevant interface element. The problem slide uses a custom-illustrated flat design of a screen-based work scenario — no photographic "frustrated business owner" cliché. Data visualization uses clean Swiss-style information design principles: thin lines, precise labels, generous white space, direct annotation.

## 7. OUTCOME

The restructured NovaPay pitch deck was delivered eleven days after the APEX engagement began. The founding team used it to approach eighteen investors across the DACH region — a mix of Swiss angel syndicates, one Berlin-based early-stage VC with an established FinTech portfolio, and two family offices with existing Swiss SME investments.

Within the first seven days of outreach, the deck generated five investor meeting requests. By the end of week three, NovaPay had completed first-round conversations with eight investors and received two term sheet indications. The CHF 2.5M round closed twelve weeks after the deck was finalized, led by a Zürich-based angel syndicate with deep Swiss banking relationships.

The founding team credited three elements specifically in post-close feedback: the opening problem frame (three investors mentioned the Saturday-evening reconciliation scenario unprompted in follow-up emails), the rigor of the market sizing methodology, and the visual simplicity of the traction slide. One investor wrote in their follow-up note: "This was one of the cleaner Pre-Seed decks I have seen out of Switzerland this year."

## 8. WHAT MAKES THIS SHOWCASE IMPRESSIVE

**Strategic Decision 1: The story opens in a kitchen, not a boardroom.**
The decision to anchor the problem slide in a specific human moment — not a market statistic — was counterintuitive for a B2B FinTech pitch. Most B2B decks lead with industry numbers because founders assume that credibility requires data first. APEX argued the opposite: data lands harder when the investor is already emotionally present in the problem. The Saturday-night reconciliation scenario did not just describe the pain — it made the investor feel the cost of inaction. That feeling is what creates urgency. Statistics inform. Stories persuade.

**Strategic Decision 2: Traction was positioned as the movie's third act, not its setup.**
Conventional pitch decks treat traction as a data slide dropped somewhere in the middle of the deck. APEX treated traction as the emotional resolution the investor had been waiting for. By sequencing problem → market → solution → product → business model → traction, each prior slide raised the stakes. When the growth chart appeared on slide 7, the investor was already primed to receive it as proof, not presentation. The chart did not need to work as hard because the narrative had done the preparation.

**Strategic Decision 3: The team slide was rewritten as an argument, not a biography.**
Most team slides list credentials. The NovaPay team slide was structured as a logical proof: given that the core challenge in Swiss SME payments sits at the intersection of banking regulation, API infrastructure, and enterprise sales — and given that these four people have each operated at the frontier of exactly one of those domains — there is a combinatorial case that this specific team cannot be easily replicated. The slide did not ask the investor to trust the team. It invited them to follow the logic.
