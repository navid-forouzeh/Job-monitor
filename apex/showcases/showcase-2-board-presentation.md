# Showcase 02 — Board Presentation
## Meridian Health Technologies GmbH · Q1 2026 Board Update · 45 Minutes

**Type:** Quarterly Board of Directors Presentation
**Stage:** Series B (CHF 28M raised)
**Industry:** Digital Health / Hospital Workflow SaaS
**Employees:** 85
**Markets:** Switzerland, Germany, Austria
**Engagement:** Board Communication Architecture · Narrative Reframing · Presentation System

---

## 1. CLIENT CONTEXT

Meridian Health Technologies GmbH is a Swiss digital health company headquartered in Basel, with additional offices in Munich and Vienna. Founded in 2019 by two physicians and a healthcare systems engineer, the company builds SaaS tools that optimize hospital workflow — specifically scheduling, resource allocation, and interdepartmental coordination — for mid-sized European hospital groups. By Q1 2026, Meridian had completed a Series B round of CHF 28M, grown to 85 employees, and established recurring contracts with eleven hospital groups across Switzerland, Germany, and Austria. Annual recurring revenue stood at CHF 7.4M with net revenue retention above 115%.

The company's board of directors includes five members: two representatives from the lead Series B VC fund (one Partner, one Principal), one former Chief Digital Officer from Roche who joined as an independent board member following the Series B, and the two co-founding physicians who retain board seats. Meridian had been running quarterly board meetings since Series A but had never applied any deliberate architecture to how those meetings were structured, how information was sequenced, or what the board was actually being asked to do. That oversight came with a cost.

---

## 2. THE CHALLENGE

Board presentations are not investor pitches. The relationship is fundamentally different: investors in a board meeting are not evaluating whether to commit capital — they have already done that. They are there to govern, advise, and hold the management team accountable to commitments made at the last meeting. A board presentation that reads like an investor pitch signals that management does not understand the governance dynamic. It creates friction, not confidence.

Meridian's pre-APEX board presentations had three chronic problems.

**Problem 1: Too operational, too deep, too early.** The existing decks ran 35–40 slides and opened with detailed sprint logs from the engineering team, weekly support ticket volumes, and granular sales activity reports. Board members — particularly the Roche CDO and the VC Partner — were senior operators who needed strategic orientation before operational data, not the other way around. Opening a board meeting with support ticket counts is the governance equivalent of meeting the CEO for lunch and discussing expense reports. The sequencing was backwards.

**Problem 2: No single message.** Board members arrived at Meridian's quarterly meetings without knowing what the single most important question for the company was that quarter. Each slide presented information, but no slide synthesized what that information meant and what the board should therefore think, decide, or advise on. Information without synthesis is noise. The board was spending the first twenty minutes of every meeting orienting themselves rather than engaging.

**Problem 3: Risk was buried.** Meridian had real strategic risks in Q1 2026 — a competitive threat from a better-funded German competitor entering the Swiss market, a key enterprise contract renewal at risk in Germany, and a hiring shortfall in engineering. None of these appeared clearly and early in the existing presentations. They were disclosed in passing, on page 28, after thirty minutes of positive performance data. This is exactly backwards: boards need risks surfaced early and clearly so they can provide counsel. Burying risks communicates either poor judgment or an attempt to soften bad news with good news first — both of which erode board trust.

---

## 3. APEX APPROACH

The core reframing decision for Meridian was architectural: shift the board presentation from an information delivery event to a governance event. These are different things. An information delivery event asks the board to receive and process data. A governance event asks the board to engage with decisions, risks, and strategic priorities. APEX rebuilt the Meridian deck around three governing principles.

**Principle 1: Executive Summary First, Always.** Every Meridian board deck now opens with a single-page executive summary that can be read in 90 seconds. It answers four questions: Where are we vs. plan? What are the one or two most important things that happened this quarter? What are the one or two most important risks the board should be aware of? And what does management need from this meeting? Board members arrive oriented. The meeting starts at a strategic level, not an operational one.

**Principle 2: Risks Belong in the First Third.** Strategic risks were moved to slide 8 in the restructured deck — before the financial deep dive, before the detailed customer metrics, and certainly before any discussion of Q2 priorities. This was counterintuitive to the Meridian leadership team, who feared leading with risks would make the board anxious. APEX argued the opposite: surfacing risks early demonstrates management maturity. It says "we know what we don't know, and we have a view on it." That is what high-performing boards want to hear. A leadership team that surfaces risk proactively earns more trust than one that manages optics.

**Principle 3: The Board Has a Job to Do.** Each section of the deck was rewritten to have a clear "board ask" — not just information, but a specific thing the board was being invited to engage with. On the competitive landscape slide, the ask was: "We'd value your perspective on how to price against Nexus Health in the German market." On the hiring slide: "We need board help identifying two senior engineering candidates in the Munich market." The deck was transformed from a reporting document into a working document. This is the distinction between boards that feel productive and boards that feel like status updates.

## 4. PRESENTATION OUTLINE

*Format: 18 slides · 45-minute meeting · 25 minutes presentation, 20 minutes discussion*

---

**Slide 1 — Executive Summary**
One slide. Four quadrants: Performance vs. Plan (traffic light — green/amber/red), Top 2 Highlights, Top 2 Risks, and Board Ask for this meeting. Readable in 90 seconds. The board knows where they are before anyone says a word. This slide is distributed 48 hours before the meeting so board members arrive prepared rather than orienting in real time.

**Slide 2 — Q1 Highlights**
Three to four headline achievements from the quarter, framed for strategic significance rather than operational detail. Not "we released 14 product features" but "we completed the integration with the SAP Health module, which unlocks our pipeline with German hospital groups requiring SAP compatibility." Each highlight is one sentence and connects to the company's strategic objectives. No bullet-point lists of activities.

**Slide 3 — Revenue Performance vs. Plan**
A single clean chart: ARR trajectory for Q1 2026 vs. the board-approved plan. Actual: CHF 7.4M ARR. Plan: CHF 7.1M ARR. Delta: +4.2% vs. plan. A secondary metric below: MRR growth rate (8% MoM) and net new ARR in Q1 (CHF 440K). One paragraph of management commentary explaining the outperformance — not celebrating it, but explaining what drove it and what is not repeatable. Boards distrust pure celebration. They want the analysis.

**Slide 4 — Customer Metrics: NPS, Churn, Expansion**
Three metrics in three panels. NPS: 68 (up from 61 in Q4 2025) — with a one-line note on what changed in the product or CS process that drove it. Gross Churn: 0% this quarter (one contract renewed early, one expanded). Net Revenue Retention: 118% — with a breakdown of expansion revenue by account (three accounts expanded scope, one added a second hospital site). The framing: customer health is the leading indicator. Revenue is the lagging one. The board needs to see both.

**Slide 5 — Product Roadmap Update**
A simplified roadmap view: what was committed to the board in Q4, what was delivered, and what slipped. No engineering sprint detail. Three rows: Delivered on time, Delivered late (with reason), Not delivered (with revised date and reason). Below the roadmap: the product team's one-line view on Q2 priority — the integration with the Austrian hospital procurement system, which is on the critical path for two pending contracts. The board does not need a feature list. They need to know if the product team is executing and what the consequences of slippage are.

**Slide 6 — Market Dynamics**
One slide on the competitive landscape — not a full analysis, but a management signal to the board about what changed in the market this quarter. Key update: Nexus Health Technologies, a Munich-based competitor with EUR 45M in Series C funding, has publicly announced entry into the Swiss hospital market. Management's current view: Nexus competes on price in the public hospital segment; Meridian's strength is in private and semi-private hospital groups where workflow complexity is higher and switching costs favor the incumbent. Board ask: does the VC Partner's portfolio have any intelligence on Nexus's go-to-market timeline in Switzerland?

**Slide 7 — Team and Hiring Update**
Current headcount: 85. Q1 additions: 6 (3 engineering, 2 sales, 1 customer success). Open roles: 8. Critical gap: two senior backend engineers in Munich — pipeline is thin. Q2 target: close 5 of the 8 open roles. One flag on team health: eNPS (employee NPS) dropped from 72 to 61 in Q1 — management has identified a root cause (engineering team burnout from a compressed Q4 delivery cycle) and has specific actions underway. The board needs to know about this before it becomes a retention problem.

**Slide 8 — Strategic Risks**
A risk register in simple format: four risks, each with a likelihood rating (High/Medium/Low), an impact rating (High/Medium/Low), and a one-line mitigation or monitoring plan. Risk 1: Nexus Health entry into Swiss market (Medium likelihood, High impact — monitored via quarterly competitive scan). Risk 2: German enterprise contract renewal at risk — Stadtspital München, contract up in August, key champion left the organization (High likelihood of complication, High impact — CEO engagement plan underway). Risk 3: Engineering hiring shortfall delays Q2 product milestones (High likelihood, Medium impact — mitigation: contractor backfill approved). Risk 4: Swiss regulatory update on hospital data sovereignty (Low likelihood, High impact — legal review commissioned). This slide is not a crisis signal. It is a management maturity signal.

**Slide 9 — Financial Deep Dive: P&L Overview**
A single P&L summary for Q1 2026: Revenue (CHF 1.85M), Gross Margin (72%), Operating Expenses (CHF 2.1M), Operating Loss (CHF -250K), Cash position (CHF 14.2M). Comparison to Q4 2025 and to Q1 plan side by side. Management commentary on gross margin improvement (up from 68% in Q4 — driven by infrastructure cost optimization) and on OpEx trajectory (on track for break-even in Q4 2026 at current growth rate). One sentence on burn rate: "At current burn, we have 19 months of runway without additional capital."

**Slide 10 — Financial Deep Dive: Unit Economics**
Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC): CHF 28K per enterprise customer (down from CHF 34K in 2025 — improved sales cycle efficiency). Average Contract Value (ACV): CHF 185K. Customer Lifetime Value (LTV): CHF 740K at current retention rates. LTV/CAC ratio: 26x. Payback period: 18 months. The message: the unit economics are strong and improving. Each new enterprise customer is a high-quality, long-duration asset.

**Slide 11 — Financial Deep Dive: Cash Flow and Runway**
A waterfall chart showing cash movements in Q1: opening balance, customer payments received, operating expenses, capex, and closing balance. Runway calculation at two burn scenarios: current burn (19 months) and accelerated-growth burn scenario for a hypothetical Q3 fundraise (12 months). No fundraise is currently planned, but the board receives this analysis quarterly. One sentence: "We are not capital-constrained at current growth rates, but we are monitoring inbound VC interest for a potential Series C in 18 months."

**Slide 12 — Q2 Priorities**
Three strategic priorities for Q2, each with an owner name, a measurable success metric, and a board review date. Priority 1: Close the Stadtspital München renewal (Owner: CEO, Target: signed by June 30, Board review: Q2 board meeting). Priority 2: Hire 2 senior Munich engineers (Owner: CPO, Target: offers accepted by May 31). Priority 3: Complete Austrian procurement system integration (Owner: CTO, Target: beta with one client by July 15). This slide converts the board presentation into an accountability mechanism. The Q2 board meeting will open by reviewing these three items first.

**Slide 13 — Open Discussion Topics**
Three agenda items for the board discussion period, pre-set by management and distributed with the deck 48 hours in advance. Topic A: Competitive response strategy for Nexus Health market entry — management will share three response options and ask the board to weigh in. Topic B: Engineering talent strategy — is the Meridian compensation band competitive in the Munich market, and can the board help with network introductions? Topic C: Series C timing and market conditions — a 10-minute pulse check on the VC Partner's view of the digital health funding environment in H2 2026. Each topic has a suggested time allocation and a clear question the board is being asked to engage with.

## 5. NARRATIVE ARCHITECTURE NOTE

Board presentations have a different narrative logic than investor pitches. The investor pitch asks: "do you believe?" The board presentation asks: "are we on track, and what do we do about what isn't?" The Meridian deck was structured around what APEX calls the Orient–Assess–Act sequence.

**Orient (Slides 1–2):** The executive summary and highlights exist to give every board member, regardless of how much prep time they had, a shared orientation in the first five minutes. The goal is to synchronize the room before any analysis begins. A board meeting where half the members are still processing the cover sheet at slide 4 is a lost meeting.

**Assess (Slides 3–8):** The core of the presentation is a balanced review of evidence — positive (revenue, customers, product) and challenging (risks, hiring gaps, competitive dynamics) — sequenced so that boards engage with the full picture before forming a view. Risk on slide 8 is early enough to inform the discussion, but late enough that the board has the context to evaluate it accurately.

**Act (Slides 9–13):** The financial deep dive and Q2 priorities convert the assessment into accountability. Board members leave knowing: what the numbers say, what management is committed to doing next quarter, and what the board's role is in making that happen. The discussion topics on slide 13 ensure that the final 20 minutes of the meeting are productive advisory time, not an open-ended Q&A that loses direction.

The overall tone of the deck is calibrated for the audience: the Roche CDO and VC Partner are senior operators who can sense when management is managing optics vs. managing the business. Every word in the deck was tested against the question: "Does this sentence communicate reality, or does it communicate how we want reality to appear?" The second type of sentence was removed.

## 6. VISUAL STYLE DIRECTION

**Color Palette:** Near-white (#FAFAFA) as the slide background. Charcoal (#1C1C1E) for all text. The primary accent is a single institutional blue (#1B4F8A) used for headers, key metrics, and chart emphasis. A secondary muted green (#2E7D32) for positive performance indicators and a muted red-amber (#C62828) for risk indicators. No decorative colors. The palette signals institutional seriousness — this is a governance document, not a marketing asset.

**Typography:** A conservative, readable serif or semi-serif for section headers (Lyon Text, or a high-quality equivalent) to communicate institutional weight. Clean sans-serif (Neue Haas Grotesk) for data labels, body copy, and numerical callouts. All font sizes are generous — nothing below 14pt in the body. Tables use tight but readable spacing with alternating row shading in light grey.

**Density:** Medium-high on data slides (financial deep dive), deliberately low on strategic slides (risks, Q2 priorities, discussion topics). The contrast in density is intentional — it helps board members shift cognitive mode between receiving information and engaging strategically. Charts use direct labeling with no legends. Tables are formatted for readability, not exhaustiveness — line-item detail lives in the appendix.

**Image Approach:** No photography except the company logo. All visuals are data visualization: charts, tables, simple process diagrams, and traffic-light status indicators. No icons except where a simple visual replaces a word (green check, amber circle, red flag for risk ratings). The design communicates that this is a serious working document prepared by a management team that respects the board's time.

## 7. OUTCOME

The restructured Q1 2026 board presentation was the first Meridian board meeting that ended on schedule. Previous meetings had consistently run 20–30 minutes over time, with the final agenda items (Q2 priorities and open discussion) consistently dropped. With the new structure, the first 25 minutes covered slides 1–13, the board discussion ran from minute 25 to minute 45, and all three open discussion topics were addressed.

Post-meeting feedback from the board was collected informally. The Roche CDO specifically commented that the risk section — appearing early and clearly — was "exactly how a well-run board meeting should feel." The VC Partner noted that the pre-distributed executive summary meant she arrived at the meeting with questions prepared rather than spending the first fifteen minutes orienting. The Munich engineering hiring gap was flagged in the meeting; two weeks later, the VC Partner had introduced Meridian's CPO to a senior engineering candidate from a portfolio company exit. That introduction led to a hire.

The Stadtspital München contract risk, surfaced clearly in the risk register, became a board-level priority. The CEO was coached by the Roche CDO on how to re-engage the hospital's new digital transformation lead — a relationship the CDO was able to facilitate. The contract was renewed in July.

---

## 8. KEY DIFFERENTIATOR: WHAT MADE THIS BOARD PRESENTATION BETTER

The single most important structural decision APEX made for the Meridian board presentation was the addition of the pre-distributed executive summary with the explicit "Board Ask" section. Most management teams prepare board decks. Very few prepare board decks that tell the board what, specifically, they are there to do. The result of adding that section was not just a better meeting — it was a different kind of board relationship. The board moved from passive receivers of information to active contributors to outcomes. That is the highest-leverage thing a management team can do with a governance document: turn it into a collaboration tool.
